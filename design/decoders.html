How the decoders work

All four decoders are token-passing algorithms on the decoding FST: at each frame, every active state holds a token (the best cost so far plus a backpointer); emitting arcs extend tokens to the next frame, epsilon arcs are consumed within the frame, and a beam prunes hopeless states.

Why token passing

The decoding graph HCLG is a transducer whose input labels are transition-ids and whose arcs already carry graph costs (LM + transition probabilities). Acoustic scores arrive per frame, so the total cost of a path is graph_cost + acoustic_cost accumulated left to right -- exactly the tropical semiring shortest-path problem, solved online by dynamic programming (Viterbi). Token passing keeps only the best token per state, which is sufficient for the 1-best and, with extra bookkeeping, for lattices.

Beam pruning

beam is subtracted from the best cost of the frame to form the pruning threshold: any token worse than best + beam dies. max_active / min_active bound the number of surviving tokens (the decoder adapts the effective beam to hit the bounds). This makes cost -- not graph size -- the dominant factor.

Emitting vs non-emitting steps

One "frame" of decoding is: ProcessEmitting (consume one frame of acoustic scores via transition-id arcs) followed by ProcessNonemitting (closure over epsilon arcs). The final costs of a frame's tokens are only complete after the non-emitting closure, which is why GetBestPath after the last frame may consult final-probs (use_final_probs).

Self-loops

HMM self-loops are ordinary arcs in the graph (inserted by kaldi's add-self-loops). FasterDecoder assumes the graph is self-loop-free and is fastest; LatticeFasterDecoder / LatticeSimpleDecoder handle general graphs.

Lattice decoders: forward links

To output a lattice (alternatives), a token must remember more than its best predecessor: every near-best predecessor within lattice_beam becomes a forward link. LatticeFasterDecoder maintains these links per token and prunes them lazily (PruneForwardLinks/PruneTokensForFrame), with final links computed at the end (FinalizeDecoding) so that final-probs are accounted exactly once. Cost offsets (cost_offsets_) renormalize per-frame costs to keep floats in range during long utterances.

Why two beams

beam controls how many states stay alive (speed/accuracy of the 1-best); lattice_beam controls how much detail the output lattice keeps. Keeping them separate lets you decode fast but still dump a rich lattice (or vice versa).

GetBestPath semantics

GetBestPath returns the DECODE-TIME best path (the Viterbi winner under the costs the decoder saw). This is what kaldi writes to its words and alignment tables in gmm-latgen-faster. Scoring a lattice under different lm weights is a separate step (Lattice weights / lattice_best_path).

These designs all come straight from kaldi; see src/decoder/lattice-faster-decoder.cc and kaldi's doc/hmm.dox for the original descriptions.
